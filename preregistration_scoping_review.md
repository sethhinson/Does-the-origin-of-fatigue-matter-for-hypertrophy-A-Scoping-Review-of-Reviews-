## Study Information

### Research Question

Does the origin of fatigue matter for hypertrophy?

### Objective

- Map the currently available reviews comparing muscle hypertrophy between training interventions that acutely increase performance fatigue or perceived fatigue and a traditional resistance training intervention.

This scoping review will systematically search and narratively synthesize reviews of resistance training research investigating differences in muscle hypertrophy between training interventions that increase acute intraset/intrasession fatigue and a traditional or control training condition. Acute fatigue was operationally defined as performance decrement and/or an increase in perceived fatigue. Various interventions have been shown to produce greater acute training fatigue than matched traditional conditions across several domains, including lower relative loads, blood flow restriction, drop sets, repetition tempo, supersets, exercise order, and pre-exhaustion (Varela-Olalla et al., 2025; Hill et al., 2022; Fink et al., 2017; Matuszczyk et al., 2026; Zhang et al., 2025; Trindade et al., 2022). Given the available acute data, it was determined that reviews investigating training density manipulations (rest interval duration, drop sets, rest-pause/myo-reps, supersets, exercise order, pre-exhaustion), relative load, blood flow restriction, and repetition tempo would qualify.

Proximity to failure and set volume will also be included under the same inclusion criteria. Closer proximities to failure and greater set volume consistently increase acute performance decrements and perceived fatigue in resistance training research (Vieira et al., 2021; Refalo et al., 2022; Varela-Olalla et al., 2025). It is important to note that both training variables elevate acute training fatigue and have dose-response relationships with muscle hypertrophy. Therefore, including reviews that investigate the effects of proximity to failure and set volume on muscle hypertrophy can inform the scoping review about the relationship between training fatigue’s various manifestations and muscle hypertrophy.

### Hypotheses

Not applicable. This is a scoping review of reviews. The objective above guides data mapping and narrative synthesis rather than hypothesis testing.

## Design Plan

### Study Type

Scoping review of reviews.

### Blinding

No blinding is involved in this study. Reviewers are not blinded to the author, journal, or institution during screening or extraction.

### Study Design

This is a scoping review of reviews guided by PRISMA-ScR (Tricco et al., 2018). The unit of inclusion is a published review (narrative review, systematic review, meta-analysis, scoping review, or umbrella review) that compares direct and indirect measures of muscle hypertrophy between training interventions that have been reported to acutely increase intraset/intrasession performance decrements or perceptions of fatigue and a traditional condition. The justification for searching for these heterogeneous studies is to map the available literature that could inform us how training fatigue may influence hypertrophy.

Included reviews are classified by synthesis type at full-text screening. Reviews are either quantitative (reports a pooled effect size: meta-analysis, network meta-analysis, or meta-regression) or narrative (systematic search conducted but synthesized narratively, or a narrative review with no formal pooling). This classification determines only which extraction fields apply and does not gate eligibility.

## Sampling Plan

### Existing Data

Registration prior to creation of data. No primary data collection is involved; all data will be drawn from previously published reviews, meta-analyses, and systematic reviews.

### Data Collection Procedures

Screening and (where applicable) extraction will be managed in Covidence. Search term development and validation will use The Evidence Review Accelerator (TERA, Institute for Evidence-Based Healthcare; https://tera-tools.com/), following the approach used by Jukic et al. (2022). The search strategy was built in the following sequence, so that every term is traceable to its source and the process is fully replicable:

1. **Seed review selection:** One review/meta-analysis per candidate intervention domain (see Seed Reviews) was identified on the basis of reviewing literature investigating muscle hypertrophy between a training protocol that leads to greater acute fatigue/performance-decrement compared to a traditional resistance training program.
2. **Word frequency generation:** The seed set was uploaded to TERA's word-frequency tool to generate candidate search terms grounded in the seed studies' title/abstract language.
3. **Secondary rule:** For seed reviews where TERA word-frequency data could not be generated or where a candidate term failed to clear the frequency report’s display threshold, the seed’s title was read directly to confirm or supply search terms.
4. **Term refinement:** TERA’s Search Strategy Refiner was used to benchmark recall and precision of the candidate string against the seed set. This aided in identifying which low-yield or redundant terms could be removed without losing seed-study recall.
5. **Cross-database translation:** The finalized PubMed string was translated into correct syntax for SPORTDiscus and SCOPUS using TERA’s Polyglot tool.

**Databases:** PubMed/MEDLINE, SPORTDiscus, SCOPUS, and Google Scholar.

Forward and backward citation tracking will be performed on the seed studies and included-review set. This is treated as a distinct, complementary search method from the database searches above, not as a substitute keyword search executed through Google Scholar's search box.

**PICO Eligibility Criteria**

| Element | Criterion |
|---|---|
| Population | Healthy adults, ages 18–55, covered by the included reviews' primary literature |
| Intervention | A manipulation of a primary resistance training variable that increases intraset/intrasession performance decrement or perceived effort relative to a traditional condition (e.g., drop sets, rest-pause/myo-reps, rest interval duration, relative load, rep tempo, exercise order, pre-exhaustion, supersets, blood flow restriction, proximity to failure, set volume) |
| Comparator | At least two protocols compared within the review, where one arm reflects the manipulated training variable and the other reflects a traditional/control resistance training condition, with all variables matched besides the independent variable(s) |
| Outcome | Muscle hypertrophy, including direct measures (muscle biopsy, ultrasound, magnetic resonance imaging, computed tomography) and indirect measures (lean mass, fat free mass, limb circumference, limb girth) |
| Article types | Narrative review, systematic review, meta-analysis, scoping review, or umbrella review |
| Publication window | 2011–2026 |
| Language | English |

Records will be imported into Covidence and independently screened by three reviewers at title/abstract and full-text stages, with conflicts resolved by discussion or a third reviewer, consistent with PRISMA-ScR reporting items on study selection.

### Data Extraction

For each included review, the following will be extracted:

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Type of review | Narrative review, systematic review, meta-analysis, scoping review, or umbrella review |
| Synthesis type | Quantitative (pooled effect size reported) or narrative (no pooling) |
| Type of intervention | The primary resistance training variable the review addresses (e.g., drop sets, rest-pause/myo-reps, rest interval, proximity to failure, relative load, rep tempo, exercise order, pre-exhaustion, supersets, set volume, blood flow restriction) |
| Measure of hypertrophy | Method(s) by which hypertrophy was assessed across the review's primary studies |
| Direction of effect | Favors the manipulated condition / favors the traditional condition / no difference / mixed |
| Magnitude and precision (quantitative reviews only) | Pooled effect size and its confidence/credible interval, where reported |
| Most-updated flag | Marked for the most recent/comprehensive meta-analysis within an intervention domain |

Not applicable. All extracted data will be narratively summarized.

Extraction will be performed in Covidence by three reviewers in continuous consultation. Charted data are inductively categorized by manipulated variable (IV domain) and narratively summarized within and across categories, following the discussion structure used by Schaller et al. (2024).
